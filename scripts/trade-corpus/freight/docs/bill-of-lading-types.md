# Bill of Lading Types — Straight, Order, Master, House, Switch and Telex Release

## By consignment method

| Type | How it reads | Transfer | Delivery requires |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Straight B/L | "Consigned to X" (named) | Not endorsed freely (non-negotiable in practice) | Identification of the named consignee |
| Order B/L | "To order [of shipper/bank]" | By endorsement + delivery of an original | Presentation of an endorsed original |
| Bearer B/L | Delivered to bearer | By hand | Presentation of an original |

Order bills are the classic negotiable instrument used under letters of credit, where the bank holds title through the endorsement chain until payment or acceptance.

## By issuer — master vs house

- **Master bill of lading (MBL)** — issued by the ocean carrier to the booking party. When a freight forwarder/NVOCC consolidates cargo, the MBL shows the forwarder as shipper and its destination agent as consignee.
- **House bill of lading (HBL)** — issued by the forwarder/NVOCC to the actual merchant (exporter/importer). The HBL mirrors the MBL data but the forwarder is liable as carrier to the house shipper. At destination the forwarder's agent collects the original HBL, releases cargo against it, and itself presents/telexes the master documents.

## Special forms and procedures

- **Sea waybill** — non-negotiable, no document of title; delivery to the identified consignee without presentation of an original. Preferred for trusted/intra-company flows and fast port turns (see the Sea waybill reference).
- **Telex release** — the carrier issues the original B/L at origin, the shipper surrenders it back to the carrier's origin office, and the carrier sends a digital message (the telex) to the destination office authorizing release without presentation of originals. Used when the shipper does not need to control title in transit, eliminating courier risk of lost originals.
- **Switch B/L** — a second set issued to replace the first (different shipper/consignee or corrected data), used in triangular trade; the original set must be surrendered and cancelled to preserve the single-document-of-title principle. Carriers restrict the practice; some jurisdictions restrict or prohibit it.
- **Stale B/L** — presented at destination later than the period stipulated (e.g., 21 days after issuance under some credit terms); banks reject stale documents under UCP 600 practice.
- **Clean vs claused/foul** — a clean B/L has no adverse notation on cargo condition; a foul B/L notes shortage/damage and is unacceptable under documentary credits.
- **On-board vs received-for-shipment** — "shipped on board" confirms loading; "received for shipment" only confirms receipt before loading (UCP 600 requires an on-board notation for port-to-port credits).
- **Long form / short form** — long form prints the full terms and conditions on the reverse; short form incorporates them by reference.

## Choosing between them

| Situation | Usual instrument |
| --- | --- |
| Payment by letter of credit | Order (negotiable) B/L, clean, on-board |
| Open account / trusted buyer, speed matters | Sea waybill or telex release |
| Intra-company transfer | Sea waybill |
| Triangular resale in transit | Switch B/L (if permitted) |
| NVOCC consolidation | HBL to merchant, MBL behind it |
