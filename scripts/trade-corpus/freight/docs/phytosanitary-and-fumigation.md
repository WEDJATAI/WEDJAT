# Phytosanitary Certificate and Fumigation / ISPM 15 Compliance

Two distinct phytosanitary instruments protect plant health in trade: the **phytosanitary certificate** (for the plants/plant products themselves) and **ISPM 15 treatment of wood packaging material** (for the crates, pallets and dunnage around the cargo). A **fumigation certificate** evidences either treatment where required.

## Phytosanitary certificate

Phytosanitary certification verifies **phytosanitary worthiness (plant health)**. The certificate attests that consignments meet the phytosanitary import requirements of the destination country.

- **Issuer**: a **National Plant Protection Organization (NPPO)** — under the WTO SPS Agreement and the IPPC's ISPM framework, a certificate for export or re-export can be issued only by a public officer technically qualified and duly authorized by an NPPO. It is usually issued by the NPPO of the country where the plants, plant products, or regulated articles were **grown or processed**.
- **Model certificates**: certificates must conform to the certifying statement of the appropriate IPPC model certificate (export or re-export).
- **US example**: the designated NPPO is USDA APHIS Plant Protection and Quarantine (PPQ); APHIS issues internationally recognized phytosanitary certificates as a service to exporters based on the foreign country's requirements, through Authorized Certification Officials (ACOs). APHIS will **not** issue a certificate for wood packaging material (pallets/crates) unless the WPM itself is the cargo.

## ISPM 15 — wood packaging material

ISPM 15 (International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15) is the IPPC measure for **wood materials thicker than 6 mm** used to ship products between countries (pallets, crates, dunnage). Purpose: prevent international transport of disease and insects that could affect plants or ecosystems.

Requirements:

1. **Debark** the wood (bark pieces allowed only if <3 cm wide, or >3 cm wide but <50 cm² total surface per piece, per the 2009 revision).
2. **Treat** by either:
   - **Heat treatment (HT)** to the standard's core-temperature/time schedule, or
   - **Fumigation with methyl bromide (MB)**.
3. **Mark** the treated wood — stamped/branded with the IPPC compliance mark (colloquially the "wheat stamp"): the IPPC certification symbol plus the treatment code (HT/MB), country code, and the producer's unique certification number issued under the NPPO's oversight, making the material traceable back to the auditing agency.

**Exemptions**: packaging made from alternative material (paper, plastic) or from wood panel products (OSB, hardboard, plywood) is exempt.

## Fumigation certificate

Where a consignment (or its packaging, or the container itself) requires fumigation — for phytosanitary, pest-control or destination-rule reasons — a **fumigation certificate** states the fumigant used (e.g. methyl bromide, phosphine), dosage, exposure duration, temperature and the treating company/authority. Destination customs and port health authorities require it with the import documents for regulated goods. Fumigation is pest control by gaseous pesticide; for containerized cargo it is typically performed before gate-in.

## Practice checklist for exporters

- Plants, plant products, some foodstuffs → obtain an NPPO **phytosanitary certificate** for export; re-exported goods need the re-export certificate.
- Any wooden pallet/crate/dunnage → ensure it is **ISPM 15 stamped** (HT or MB mark visible on two opposite sides).
- Destination-specific treatment rules (e.g. certain countries requiring fumigation of the whole container) → arrange and certificate the fumigation before export.
- Missing stamps or missing certificates are among the most common causes of import quarantine refusal or re-export orders.
