# Pre-Shipment Inspection and Conformity Certificates

**Pre-shipment inspection (PSI)** is a supply-chain quality-control method for checking the quality of goods buyers purchase from suppliers before the goods are shipped. Governments also mandate PSI/conformity programmes for import control (so-called "conformity assessment" regimes), in which case the inspection certificate becomes a **customs prerequisite** at destination.

## Commercial PSI

- **Purpose**: ensure production complies with the governing specification, contract or purchase order.
- **Final random inspection (FRI)**: performed when at least 80% of the order is produced and export-packed; samples selected at random per the inspection standard.
- **Standard**: ANSI/ASQC Z1.4 (ISO 2859-1) governs the sampling process; the **Acceptable Quality Limit (AQL)** is agreed between importer and manufacturer and is a necessary part of the contract.
- **Role in payment**: pre-shipment acceptance sampling may be agreed between buyer, supplier and a bank and used to initiate payment under a letter of credit.
- **Stages inspected**: quantity and packing, quality/consistency, documentation (test reports, packing list), compliance with destination-country standards (e.g. ASME, CE marking) and import regulations.
- **Providers**: privately-owned free-market inspection companies (the buyer's risk: small companies paid by the manufacturer may serve the manufacturer's interest) and a few state-owned/partly state-owned companies.

## Mandatory conformity-for-import programmes

Many importing states require a **Certificate of Conformity (CoC)** issued by an appointed inspection agency before customs clearance — typically covering safety/quality standards for consumer and industrial goods. Examples of such regimes (each with its own portal and process):

- **Saudi Arabia — SASO/SABER**: product conformity certification (and shipment certificates) required for regulated goods before Saudi customs clearance.
- Other Gulf/African/Latin programmes with similar mechanics (appointed verification agencies, certificate against invoice/HS data).

Mechanics in common: the exporter books inspection with the appointed agency → inspection of goods and documents → Certificate of Conformity linked to the commercial invoice and (often) a unique shipment reference → the destination customs authority verifies the certificate at clearance.

## Practice checklist

1. Check whether the destination country operates a mandatory conformity regime for the product's HS code (see the customs country guides in this corpus).
2. Book PSI early — inspections must be completed before cargo gates in; failures delay the vessel booking.
3. Align AQL and inspection standards in the sales contract where the buyer requires commercial PSI.
4. Ensure the CoC references match the final invoice/packing list exactly (quantity/value changes invalidate certificates).
5. Keep certificates digitally available at destination for the clearing agent.
