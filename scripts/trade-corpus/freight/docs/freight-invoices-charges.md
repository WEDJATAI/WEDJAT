# Ocean Freight Invoice — Charges Structure (Freight, THC, BAF/CAF, Demurrage, Detention)

The quoted base rate is only a small portion of the real cost of an ocean shipment. Carriers bill a structured set of freight charges and surcharges; understanding the structure is essential for budgeting and for comparing quotations (per SeaRates' ocean-freight cost analysis and Maersk's demurrage/detention guidance).

## Charge families

| Charge | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Base ocean freight** | The linehaul rate per container/TEU for the port-to-port move, by service contract or spot quotation |
| **Fuel surcharges (BAF, LSF, ECA)** | Bunker Adjustment Factor plus Low Sulfur Fuel and Emission Control Area charges — driven by fuel prices and the environmental zones the ship traverses (IMO sulfur regulations force low-sulfur fuel in certain zones) |
| **Currency Adjustment Factor (CAF)** | Carrier's compensation for currency fluctuation between the freight currency and the local currencies at the ends of the trade |
| **Terminal Handling Charges (THC)** | Port-terminal fees for handling containers arriving/departing; inconsistent port-to-port with different infrastructure, labour and equipment levels |
| **Documentation & administrative fees** | Bill of lading issuance (paper or e-form), release fees, amendment fees, telex/eB/L fees — individually small, cumulatively significant |
| **Transshipment/transit fees** | Transfer between vessels at an intermediate port when no direct routing exists |
| **Port storage** | Per-day charge after free time expires while cargo remains at the port; increases steeply with duration |
| **Demurrage** | Fee for **use of the container inside the port** beyond agreed free time |
| **Detention** | Fee for **retaining the container outside the port** after pick-up (until returned empty) |
| **Peak Season Surcharge (PSS)** | Applied when cargo demand peaks (holiday seasons, export booms from manufacturing countries) |
| **Special-cargo surcharges** | Oversized/project cargo, refrigerated containers, dangerous goods (IMO-regulated) — extra care, scrutiny and handling |
| **Last-mile costs** | Post-port trucking, customs clearance, unloading, door deliveries |

## Free time, demurrage, detention — the clock rules

- **Free time** determines the number of days the container can be used free of charge (commonly a few days; typical market ranges around 7–10 days but varying by carrier, route and season). Beyond it, demurrage (inside port) and detention (outside port) accrue — often escalating daily.
- On the charter side, demurrage is liquidated damages owed to the shipowner when the charterer uses the vessel for loading/unloading beyond the allowed **laytime**; its contractual inverse is **despatch** (paid by the owner for time saved). NOR (notice of readiness) timing fixes when laytime starts.
- Scheduling transport and customs to clear before free time expiry is the operational defence against these fees.

## Freight invoice practice

- Freight is stated **prepaid or collect** (Incoterm-driven — see the Incoterms reference).
- Invoices cite the booking/B/L number; verify against the quotation's surcharge list.
- Destination charges (destination THC, D/O fee, cleaning) are commonly billed to the consignee — importers should request the full destination charge breakdown in advance.

## Budgeting checklist (per SeaRates)

1. Obtain the complete written surcharge list with every quotation.
2. Create a buffer for unexpected costs (rate volatility, PSS windows, rollovers).
3. Use a budgeting checklist per shipment including last-mile fees and free-time limits.
