# Commercial Invoice — International Trade and Customs Valuation Document

The **commercial invoice** is the seller's formal demand for payment and, in foreign trade, a **customs document**: the declaration of the goods provided by the person or corporation exporting the item across international borders. Customs authorities use it to assess tariffs, and banks use it under documentary credits. There is no single standard format, but the document must include specific pieces of information.

## Required content

| Element | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Parties | Seller (exporter) and buyer (importer), full names/addresses |
| Invoice number & date | The document's identity; date matters for payment terms and credits |
| Buyer reference | PO/contract number |
| Description of goods | Plain-language description matching the packing list; **Harmonized System codes** for the goods |
| Country of manufacture | Origin of the goods |
| Quantities & units of measure | Per line and total |
| Unit prices & extended totals | Currency stated |
| Incoterms | e.g. "CIF Alexandria — Incoterms 2020" — the price basis |
| Payment terms | e.g. 30 days net, documentary credit |
| Certification & signature | A statement certifying the invoice is true, signed — often required by customs |
| (Where required) | HS codes per line, origin statement, export licence references, freight/insurance amounts when the Incoterm requires |

## Customs role

- Used to calculate tariffs and duties (with the HS classification) and to support **customs valuation** — the transaction value basis under the WTO Valuation Agreement in most systems.
- Between EU member states a commercial invoice for customs is generally not needed; it is required between the EU and non-EU countries.
- Many jurisdictions require consular legalization or chamber certification of the invoice for certain origins (see the country guides in this corpus).

## Practice notes

- The invoice should be consistent with the packing list (weights/pieces) and the B/L data; discrepancies trigger customs queries and bank refusal under credits.
- In many countries the definitive VAT/sales invoice for payment and the customs commercial invoice are distinct documents; in the EU, public-procurement invoices must be electronic (Directive 2014/55/EU).
- For preferential duty claims the invoice is paired with a certificate of origin or an origin declaration (see the Certificate of origin reference).
