# Sea Waybill — Non-Negotiable Ocean Transport Document

The **sea waybill** (ocean waybill) is a transport document that, unlike a bill of lading, is **non-negotiable and is not a document of title**. In international trade outside the United States, bills of lading are distinct from waybills in that the latter are not transferable and do not confer title. The carrier simply delivers the goods to the consignee identified in the waybill, upon proof of identity — no presentation of an original document is required.

## Function

- **Receipt** for the goods shipped and **evidence of the contract of carriage** — same two functions as a B/L.
- **No document of title** — the third B/L function is absent; this is the defining difference.
- Carriage conditions remain those of the carrier's tariff and the applicable convention (Hague/Hague-Visby/Hamburg Rules or national law, e.g. UK Carriage of Goods by Sea Act 1992, which grants rights of suit to the consignee under a sea waybill).

## When a sea waybill is preferred

| Situation | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Trusted buyer / open account terms | No need to control title pending payment |
| Intra-company shipments | Cargo never changes ownership outside the group |
| Short-sea routes where transit < courier time | Originals would arrive after the vessel |
| Fast turnaround ports | Avoids original-document queue at destination |
| Cargo unlikely to be resold in transit | No need for negotiability |

## How it works in practice

1. The shipper's shipping instructions request a waybill instead of a negotiable B/L.
2. The carrier issues the waybill (paper and/or electronic); no originals circulate.
3. On arrival, the carrier notifies the consignee (arrival notice); the consignee proves identity and collects the cargo after settling freight if collect.

## Waybill vs B/L — quick comparison

| Feature | Bill of lading (order) | Sea waybill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receipt for goods | Yes | Yes |
| Evidence of contract | Yes | Yes |
| Document of title | Yes | No |
| Delivery against | Endorsed original presented | Identity of named consignee |
| Suitable under documentary credit | Yes | Generally not (banks lose security) |
| Courier risk of lost originals | Present | Absent |

## Notes

- Waybills suit the Rotterdam Rules' category of "non-negotiable transport documents".
- Many carriers now issue electronic waybills through their platforms as the default for bookings where no original document is requested.
- If the seller needs security of payment or the goods may be traded in transit, use a negotiable B/L (see Bill of lading and Bill of lading types references).
