# Port Agents, Notice of Readiness, Laytime and Statements of Facts

## The port agent

The **port agent** (ship's agent) is the carrier's or owner's local representative at a port — arranging berth, pilotage, tugs, stevedores, supplies and documentation, and acting as the vessel's communication point with the port and authorities. The agent draws up the key time-recording documents of every call.

## Notice of Readiness (NOR)

- The **master must give a Notice of Readiness (NOR) to the charterer when the ship has arrived at the port of loading or discharge** — the NOR informs the charterer that the ship is ready to load or discharge.
- **The date and time of the NOR determines when laytime commences.**
- NOR validity (tendering before/at berth, at usual anchorage, WIBON clauses) is a classic charter-party dispute area — the tender terms are contractual.

## Laytime, demurrage and despatch

- **Laytime** is the time allowed by the charter party for loading/unloading. It applies in both liner and tramp contexts.
- **Demurrage** = liquidated damages owed by the charterer to the shipowner when the vessel stays beyond laytime (laytime consumed minus laytime allowed); it increases the cost of total freight and can cause losses to the seller.
- **Despatch** = the inverse: if the charterer uses less than the allowed laytime, the charter party may require the shipowner to pay despatch for the time saved.

## Statement of Facts and the time sheet

- **At the end of the stay in port, the port agent draws up a Statement of Facts (SOF)** — a log of events during the ship's stay in port (arrivals, berthing, work start/stop, weather interruptions, shifting, completion).
- The SOF enables a **time sheet** to be drawn up and **signed by the master and the shipper or receiver**, and the time sheet enables laytime — and therefore **demurrage or despatch — to be calculated**.

## Liner-trade relevance

In liner/container trades, merchant-facing equivalents of these time rules appear as the free-time/demurrage/detention regimes on carrier tariffs (see the Container terminals reference); the NOR/SOF machinery governs charter parties (voyage/time charters) and terminal agreements on bulk and breakbulk trades.

## Practice checklist

1. Check the charter party's laytime clause, NOR-tender conditions and demurrage rate before fixing.
2. During the call, ensure the agent's SOF accurately records interruptions attributable to weather/port (stoppages that suspend laytime per the charter terms).
3. Sign the time sheet promptly; disputes are documented against the SOF.
4. For containers, track the free-time clock — the commercial analogue of laytime.
