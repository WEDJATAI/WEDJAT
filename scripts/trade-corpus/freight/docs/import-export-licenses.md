# Import and Export Licenses and Controlled Goods

**Export control** is legislation regulating the export of goods, software and technology where the items could be used contrary to the exporting country's interests. Items so identified are **controlled**, and exporters must apply for a **licence** from a government department before shipping them. On the import side, mirror systems of **import licenses and permits** restrict who may bring in what, from where, and in what quantities.

## How control systems work

Most regimes:

1. **List the controlled items** — often harmonized through multilateral regimes (e.g. the **Wassenaar Arrangement** for conventional arms and dual-use goods).
2. **Classify the destinations** — embargoed/sanctioned countries, countries triggering licence requirements.
3. **Require exporters to apply for a licence** from the competent national authority (e.g. commerce/state/finance ministries depending on the regime).

Possible outcomes of an export assessment: no licence required (item/destination not controlled), licence required, or prohibited (sanctions/embargo).

## Categories of control

| Category | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Military use | Items "designed or modified for military use" |
| **Dual-use** | Items with **both civilian and military purpose** (much of advanced electronics, software, machine tools, chemicals) |
| Uncontrolled | Ordinary commercial goods |

Regional example: the EU operates Regulation 2021/821 on dual-use export controls; post-Brexit Great Britain runs its own regime while Northern Ireland follows the EU rules for dual-use movements (no export licences required for GB→NI dual-use movements under the Protocol).

## Typical import license systems

- **Automatic** — registration/statistical licenses (granted on request).
- **Non-automatic** — protective/health/security licensing (quotas, exchange control, product safety), e.g.:
  - Food/agriculture import permits (veterinary/phytosanitary-based — see those references).
  - Pharmaceutical/medical-device registrations.
  - Telecom/radio equipment approvals.
  - Dual-use/strategic imports and sanctioned-origin goods.
- **Trader registers** — many countries require the importing company itself to hold an importer registry card/registration before any license (see the customs country guides in this corpus).

## Documents in play

| Document | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Export licence/authorization | Government permission naming exporter, item classification, quantity, destination, end-user |
| End-user certificate/statement | Destination-side assurance of the final use/user (common for dual-use) |
| Import license/permit | Destination authority's permission to import |
| Commercial invoice | Must cite license numbers where required |
| B/L + manifests | Customs checks licence references against cargo |

## Practice checklist

1. Classify the product against the control list (dual-use list of the exporting jurisdiction) before quoting.
2. Screen counterparties and destinations against sanctions lists.
3. Apply for the export licence early (lead times vary by authority); cite it on all documents.
4. Obtain the buyer's import license before shipment where the destination requires it.
5. Keep end-use/end-user documentation on file — authorities audit after export.
