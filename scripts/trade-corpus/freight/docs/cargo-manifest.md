# Cargo Manifest and Advance Customs Filing (ISF, ENS)

A **manifest** (customs manifest or cargo document) lists the cargo, passengers and crew of a ship, aircraft or vehicle **for the use of customs and other officials**. For cargo it is the consolidated list of everything laden aboard — the goods' passport.

## Manifest vs bill of lading

| Aspect | Bill of lading | Cargo manifest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nature | Legal instrument (ownership/title, contract) | Physical/administrative list (weights, sizes, stowage) |
| Per shipment | One per contract of carriage (multiple per vessel) | One consolidated list for the whole vessel (or container) |
| Variants | — | Dangerous-cargo manifest; freight manifest (commercial, exchanged between liner agents); customs manifest (regulatory) |

The manifest is made up by the ship's broker **from the contents of the bills of lading**, containing a specification of the nature and quantity of the cargo laden, generally attested officially. The freight manifest and customs manifest differ little in content but completely in legal status.

## Customs use

Customs authorities may require cargo-carrying vessels or vehicles to provide on the manifest: consignor, consignee, quantity of goods, origin, destination and value. The customs manifest is sent to customs on arrival at the first port in a country — and increasingly **before departure/arrival electronically** under advance-filing regimes:

| Regime | Jurisdiction | Core rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Importer Security Filing "10+2" (ISF)** | United States | Importer/forwarder files 10 data elements to CBP typically **no later than 24 hours before loading** on the vessel at the foreign port; the carrier files 2 more (vessel stow plan, container status) |
| **Entry Summary Declaration (ENS)** | European Union | Lodged before arrival of goods in the EU (timing depends on the transport mode); risk analysis by customs before the goods enter |
| Similar pre-arrival filing | Many states | e.g., China, Canada and others operate AMS/advance-commercial filing regimes with carrier/carrier-agent manifests |

Missing or late ISF exposes the importer to US penalties ("do not load" holds, liquidated damages) and cargo delays.

## What the forwarder/shipper must do

1. Provide complete cargo descriptions, HS codes where required, consignee/notify data, container numbers and seal data in the shipping instructions — these flow to the carrier's manifest filings.
2. File (or instruct the filer to file) the importer security data for US-bound ocean cargo before the vessel loading deadline.
3. Ensure dangerous cargo is separately manifested (dangerous-cargo manifest with IMDG data — see the Dangerous goods documents reference).
