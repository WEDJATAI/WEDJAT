# Marine Cargo Insurance Certificate and Institute Cargo Clauses

Marine insurance covers the physical loss or damage of ships, **cargo**, terminals, and any transport by which property is transferred between origin and final destination. Cargo insurance is the sub-branch covering the goods; the **insurance certificate** is the document evidencing that cover to the buyer, the bank and customs.

## The certificate

Issued by (or on behalf of) the insurer after the policy is bound, the certificate states:

| Element | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Insured / beneficiary | Usually the exporter (transferred to buyer under CIF/CIP by endorsement) |
| Insured voyage | Vessel, voyage, POL/POD, transit period, warehouse-to-warehouse extent |
| Goods & sums insured | Invoice value plus agreed percentage (e.g. 110% of CIF under credits) |
| Clauses | Level of cover — Institute Cargo Clauses (A), (B) or (C) — plus war/strikes clauses where agreed |
| Claims details | Claim agents at destination, claim procedure, survey requirement |
| Marks & numbers | Matching the B/L and packing list |

## Institute Cargo Clauses (A), (B), (C)

In the 19th century Lloyd's and the **Institute of London Underwriters** developed standardized clauses for marine insurance, maintained since — the **Institute Clauses** (the Institute paid for their publication, hence the name). Modern policy documents typically combine the **MAR 91** general wording with the applicable Institute Clauses stapled inside.

| Level | Cover philosophy | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ICC (A)** | All-risks basis (named exclusions) | Highest commercial cover — the default required under Incoterms 2020 **CIP** |
| **ICC (B)** | Named perils | Middle cover |
| **ICC (C)** | Major named perils only (fire, explosion, vessel casualty, washing overboard, etc.) | Minimum cover — the default under Incoterms 2020 **CIF** |

Incoterms 2020 raised the CIP default from Clauses (C) to **Clauses (A)** (or similar), while CIF — reserved for maritime/commodity trade — remains on Clauses (C) unless the parties agree higher cover.

## Who insures under which Incoterm

| Incoterm | Seller's insurance duty |
| --- | --- |
| CIF, CIP | Seller must procure cargo insurance for the buyer's risk period |
| All other terms (EXW, FCA, FAS, FOB, CFR, CPT, DAP, DPU, DDP) | No insurance obligation — the buyer arranges cover (in practice the buyer or its bank requires it) |

## Core loss doctrines

- **General average (GA)** — from the Lex Rhodia onward: when a voluntary sacrifice (jettison, salvage, voluntary grounding) saves the adventure, all interests (hull/cargo/freight) contribute in proportion to value at risk. Cargo insurers issue GA guarantees.
- **Particular average** — a partial loss to hull or cargo (not GA).
- **Total loss** vs constructive total loss doctrines; hull cover may be "Total Loss Only" (TLO).
- **P&I clubs** — mutual Protection & Indemnity associations cover the shipowner's third-party liabilities (including the quarter not covered historically by hull policies, wreck removal, pollution).

## Practice notes

- Under documentary credits (UCP 600), the insurance document must be for the currency of the credit and cover at least 110% of the CIF/CIP value.
- Certificates are negotiable by endorsement when issued "to order".
- Duty calculation contexts: some jurisdictions compute import duty against the insured value (India: CIF basis; South Africa: FOB basis) — check the country guides in this corpus.
