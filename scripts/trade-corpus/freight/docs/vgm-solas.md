# Verified Gross Mass — SOLAS VI/2 Container Weighing Requirement

The **SOLAS Container Weight Verification requirement (SOLAS chapter VI, regulation 2)** — the "VGM rule" — was adopted in 2015 by the IMO Maritime Safety Committee as an amendment to the International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS). It requires that the full (gross) weight of all loaded containers must be obtained **before the container is loaded on board an ocean vessel**.

## The requirement

- No container may be loaded aboard a vessel without a **Verified Gross Mass (VGM)** communicated to the carrier.
- **The exporter (shipper) is ultimately responsible** for obtaining the verified container weight.
- Implementation began **1 July 2016**, with flexibility for practical refinement until 1 October 2016.
- Communicating the weight introduced the **VERMAS** EDI message ("Verification of Mass") — an Electronic Data Interchange protocol implemented between ocean carriers, freight forwarders/NVOCCs, EDI providers and exporters.

## The two approved methods

| Method | Description | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Method 1** | Weigh the **loaded container** (bridge/weighbridge, lifting equipment with certified load cells, axle-weight computation where approved) | Simplest where calibrated equipment exists |
| **Method 2** | Weigh **all cargo, dunnage and packaging** separately and add the **container tare weight** | Requires an approved weighing procedure; some jurisdictions restrict or disallow Method 2 for certain cargo |

## Operational practice

- Carriers publish a **VGM cut-off** per port/vessel; a container without VGM by cut-off is denied loading.
- VGM is submitted via the carrier's portal, EDI (VERMAS/FTM or VGM message), or through the terminal's VGM service (weighing at gate-in where offered).
- Shippers' VGM methods may require approval/registration with the competent authority of the shipping country.
- VGM differs from the cargo gross weight on the B/L: it is the **total** weight of container + cargo + securing material.

## Misdeclaration consequences

- Containers with **no VGM or a doubtful VGM are denied loading** until verified.
- Wrong declarations expose the shipper to liability for accidents/losses caused by misdeclared weight, and to carrier/administrative penalties.
- The rule exists because misdeclared weights have caused stack collapses, structural damage and casualties at sea.
