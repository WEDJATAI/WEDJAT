# Incoterms 2020 — All 11 Rules Reference

**Incoterms 2020** is the ninth set of international contract terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce (first set: 1936). It defines **11 rules** — the same count as 2010 — after removing "Delivered at Terminal" (**DAT**) and replacing it with "Delivered at Place Unloaded" (**DPU**). Defined meanings: **Delivery** = the point where risk of loss/damage transfers from seller to buyer; **Carrier** = any person undertaking to perform or procure transport by any mode or combination; **Terminal** = dock, warehouse, container yard or cargo terminal; **To clear for export** = file the export declaration and obtain the export permit.

## Group 1 — Rules for any mode(s) of transport

| Rule | Delivery / risk transfer | Carriage | Insurance | Export clearance | Import clearance | Key notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **EXW** Ex Works (named place) | At seller's premises when placed at buyer's disposal | Buyer | Buyer | Buyer (practical problem: many jurisdictions require the declarant to be resident — EU buyers cannot clear; FCA seller's premises often better) | Buyer | Minimum seller obligation; buyer handles everything from the door |
| **FCA** Free Carrier (named place) | When handed to the buyer's carrier/nominee at named place | Buyer | Buyer | Seller | Buyer | The 2020 FCA includes an on-board B/L option (buyer instructs carrier to issue on-board B/L to seller) — the modern container-shipping term |
| **CPT** Carriage Paid To (named destination) | When handed to the **first carrier** at origin — risk passes in the country of export | Seller pays to named destination | Buyer (CIP if seller must insure) | Seller | Buyer | Replaces C&F/CFR for all non-containerized modes |
| **CIP** Carriage and Insurance Paid to (named destination) | Same as CPT | Seller | **Seller must insure 110% of contract value under Institute Cargo Clauses (A)** (2020 raised the minimum from ICC (C)) or similar, in contract currency, claimable by buyer/seller/any insurable interest | Seller | Buyer | All modes; container-friendly |
| **DPU** Delivered at Place Unloaded (named destination) | When **unloaded** at named destination; seller bears all risk until arrival and unloading | Seller | Buyer (no obligation) | Seller | Buyer | Only rule requiring seller to unload; terminal must be able to receive the shipment; import duty/taxes/on-carriage are buyer's; demurrage at terminal generally seller's; container "unloaded" ambiguity noted |
| **DAP** Delivered At Place (named destination) | When placed at buyer's disposal **on the arriving means of transport ready for unloading** | Seller | Buyer | Seller | Buyer | Buyer clears import (permits, duties, taxes); unloading at final destination is buyer's |
| **DDP** Delivered Duty Paid (named destination) | At named place in buyer's country, not unloaded | Seller | Buyer | Seller | **Seller** (duties, taxes, registrations) | Maximum seller obligation; seller must master the buyer-country import regime — delays/unforeseen costs risk; replaces non-Incoterm "Free In Store" |

## Group 2 — Rules for sea and inland waterway transport

| Rule | Delivery / risk transfer | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **FAS** Free Alongside Ship (named port of shipment) | When placed **alongside** the buyer's vessel at the named port | 2020 reversal: **seller** clears for export (buyer only if expressly agreed); non-containerized cargo only |
| **FOB** Free on Board (named port of shipment) | When loaded **on board** the vessel | Seller arranges export clearance; buyer pays freight/insurance/on-carriage; goods must be "appropriated to the contract"; since Incoterms 1980's FCA, FOB is only proper for non-containerized sea freight (commonly misused for all modes) |
| **CFR** Cost and Freight (named port of destination) | On board in the country of export | Seller pays freight to destination port; no insurance duty (use CIF); non-containerized sea only — use CPT otherwise |
| **CIF** Cost, Insurance & Freight (named port of destination) | On board in the country of export | Seller insures; ICC **(C)** minimum clauses remain the CIF default (commodity/maritime term) unless higher agreed |

Container caveat: the four sea terms pass risk at loading on board — with sealed containers the cargo condition at that point is unverifiable, which is why FCA/CPT/CIP are the correct terms for containerized trade.

## What changed in 2020 vs 2010

1. **DAT removed → DPU** added (same mechanics, renamed, unloading obligation explicit).
2. **CIP insurance minimum raised from ICC (C) to ICC (A)**; CIF stays at (C).
3. **FCA on-board B/L option** for credit sellers.
4. Own-transport arrangements recognized (seller/buyer may use their own vehicles).
5. Security-related allocation of costs and documentation duties itemized.

## Duty-calculation usage in some jurisdictions

Some states compute duty against a specific Incoterm: **India assesses duty on the CIF value; South Africa on the FOB value** — exporters there sometimes adopt those terms even when unsuitable for the mode, requiring careful clarification of cost/risk transfer points.

## Practice pitfalls

- Name the place/port precisely (UN/LOCODE for sea rules) and the point within it.
- Variations from standard meaning must be explicit — casual adoption produces unexpected results.
- Under credits, match the Incoterm with the transport document type the bank requires.
- EXW + export clearance: in the EU the buyer usually cannot declare — prefer FCA.
