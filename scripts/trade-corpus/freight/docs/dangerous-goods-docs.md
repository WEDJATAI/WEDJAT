# Dangerous Goods Documents — IMDG Code, DG Declaration and SDS

Dangerous goods (hazardous materials, DG/HazMat) shipped by sea are governed by the **International Maritime Dangerous Goods (IMDG) Code** — the International Maritime Organization's implementation of the UN Recommendations on the Transport of Dangerous Goods. The IMDG Code's parts include classification (Part 2) and the Dangerous Goods List with Limited Quantity exceptions (Part 3); the shipper's documentary duties are the Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods, packing documentation, and marking/labelling/placarding.

## The 9 hazard classes

| Class | Hazard family |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Explosives |
| 2 | Gases |
| 3 | Flammable liquids |
| 4 | Flammable solids (and substances liable to spontaneous combustion / emit flammable gas on water) |
| 5 | Oxidizing substances and organic peroxides |
| 6 | Toxic and infectious substances |
| 7 | Radioactive material |
| 8 | Corrosive substances |
| 9 | Miscellaneous dangerous substances and articles |

(Some classes subdivide into divisions; class 7 cargoes connect to the INF Code for irradiated nuclear fuel carriage.)

## Identification system

- Every regulated substance/article carries a **UN number** and a **proper shipping name** assigned by hazard classification and composition; commonly carried dangerous goods are listed in the Code's Dangerous Goods List.
- Marking/labelling uses the UN-based system — but graphics and wording vary by country; the national transport regulations of the country of interest govern local presentation (Hazchem codes in UK/AU, for example).

## The documents

| Document | Who issues | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods (DGD)** | The shipper | Legal declaration that the goods are classified, packed, marked and labelled per IMDG; includes UN number, proper shipping name, class, packing group, quantity, container/vehicle identification, and a signed certification |
| **Container packing certificate** | The packer | Confirms the cargo transport unit was packed, segregated and secured per the Code |
| **Safety Data Sheet (SDS/MSDS)** | Product manufacturer | Hazard communication document (16-section format under GHS); required by carriers/forwarders to accept DG bookings |
| **Dangerous Goods List entry data** | Standard | EmS (emergency schedules), MFAG medical guide references, stowage & segregation instructions per substance |

## Segregation and stowage

The IMDG Code prescribes **segregation distances/rules** between incompatible classes within the ship and the container, stowage categories (on/deck, away from heat), and special provisions per UN number. The DGD and packing certificate together attest that segregation inside the unit was respected.

## Limited quantities

The Code provides **Limited Quantity exceptions** (Part 3) — small inner packagings within prescribed per-package limits, marked with the LQ mark, which reduce marking/placarding duties but never remove the classification/identification requirements.

## Process for a DG ocean shipment

1. Classify the product (class, division, UN number, packing group) from the SDS and the Dangerous Goods List.
2. Book with the carrier declaring DG data at booking stage (most carriers refuse undeclared DG discovered later).
3. Package and mark per Code; pack the container; complete the packing certificate.
4. Sign the DGD and submit with the shipping instructions; placard the unit where required.
5. Port handling, stowage and emergency response follow the vessel's DG manifest and EmS/MFAG.

Misdeclared or undeclared dangerous goods are among the leading causes of container fires; carriers and regulators apply severe penalties and cargo rejection.
