# Documents Under Letters of Credit (UCP 600)

The **letter of credit (documentary credit)** is the bank's undertaking to pay the beneficiary (exporter) against presentation of complying documents. Handling practice is standardized by the ICC **Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits** — published since 1933, with the current version **UCP 600**, approved by the ICC Banking Commission on 25 October 2006 and effective **1 July 2007** (a 2017 ICC review decided not to revise it, keeping the need "under review"). UCP 600 applies only where the credit indicates it (a SWIFT MT700 must name it in field 40E), and its 39 articles govern unless expressly modified or excluded.

## The document set

The credit names the documents required; the classic ocean set:

| Document | Typical UCP 600 examination points |
| --- | --- |
| Bill of lading (transport document) | On-board notation, clean, issued/carried within the shipment period, consigned/notified per credit, 21-day presentation window after shipment |
| Commercial invoice | Issued by the beneficiary, currency/amount consistent, description matching the credit |
| Packing list | Consistency with invoice quantities/pieces |
| Insurance document | For CIF/CIP credits: in the credit currency, minimum cover (110% of CIF/CIP value), clauses level (ICC (A)/(C)) |
| Certificate of origin | Issuing authority, origin statements per credit |
| Inspection/quality certificates | Issuer, date, sampling/standard references |
| Beneficiary certificate | Certifying dispatch of documents/notifications as instructed |

## Compliance examination

- Banks examine documents **on their face** to determine, on the basis of the documents alone, whether they comply with the credit's terms. They are not expected to verify the underlying facts (goods' quality/quantity) — the **abstraction principle**; the main exception is fraud (dishonest sellers presenting compliant-but-fraudulent documents shift the loss to the buyer, so banks scrutinize legitimacy).
- **Discrepancy handling**: if documents do not comply they are discrepant; the nominated bank informs the beneficiary and offers options (after the applicant's consent) — refusal must be based on reasonable examination identifying a material mistake (not trivialities; an early delivery date was held not material by English courts). Minor discrepancies can sometimes be cured by re-presenting corrected documents within the credit's limits. Late presentation (beyond credit/UCP time limits) is discrepant.
- The **ISBP — International Standard Banking Practice for the Examination of Documents under Documentary Credits (ICC Publication 745)** is the necessary companion for determining compliance; the 2013 revision aligned it with UCP 600.

## Categories of credits

Common variants include sight vs usance (deferred payment), confirmed vs unconfirmed, transferable, revolving, standby, and red-clause credits — each suited to different markets and risks. Banks typically require collateral from the applicant and charge a fee, often a percentage of the covered amount.

## Practice checklist for exporters

1. Check the credit against the sales contract **before shipping** (amount, Incoterm, document list, deadlines achievable?).
2. Prepare documents with data consistency across the set (B/L ↔ invoice ↔ packing list ↔ certificates).
3. Present within the credit's expiry and the 21-day transport-document window.
4. Demand drafts pre-checked by the nominated bank where possible to cure discrepancies in time.
5. Remember the bank pays **documents**, not goods — the buyer's protection is exactly that; the seller's is the bank's undertaking.
