# Arrival Notice and Delivery Order — Cargo Release at Destination

Two documents govern the import-side handover of ocean cargo: the **arrival notice** announces that the shipment has reached destination, and the **delivery order (D/O)** authorizes the terminal/carrier to hand the goods to the importer.

## Arrival notice

An **arrival notice** is issued by the carrier (or its destination agent) to the consignee, notify party and importer of record to inform them the shipment has arrived at its destination port. It typically lists:

- Vessel/voyage and discharge date, ETA of availability
- Container/cargo identification and B/L number
- Free time expiry, demurrage/detention start dates
- Location of the cargo (terminal) and required steps to obtain release
- Charges due (freight if collect, THC, documentation fees)

The arrival notice starts the clock: consignees arrange import customs clearance and pickup before free time expires (see the Container terminals reference for demurrage/detention).

## Delivery order (D/O)

A **delivery order** is a document from a consignee, or an owner, or the agent of a freight carrier which orders the release of the transportation of cargo to another party. Under the Uniform Commercial Code (US), a delivery order is "an order given by an owner of goods to a person in possession of them (the carrier or warehouseman) directing that person to deliver the goods to a person named in the order."

In ocean import practice:

1. The consignee (or its broker) presents the required documents to the carrier's destination agent — for negotiable B/Ls, **an original endorsed bill of lading** (unless telex-released); for sea waybills, identification and proof of freight payment.
2. The agent issues the **delivery order** to the terminal authorizing release to the named party.
3. The consignee presents the D/O (with customs release) at the terminal gate and collects the cargo.

A delivery order used for import should not be confused with "delivery instructions" — the specific information given to the inland carrier concerning the forwarder's arrangement to deliver the merchandise to a particular pier or steamship line.

## Telex release connection

Where the shipper surrendered the originals at origin (telex release), the destination office releases cargo on presentation-free proof of identity, and the D/O is issued without collecting an original B/L. With electronic B/L systems the surrender/endorsement is digital (see the Electronic bill of lading reference).

| Step | Document |
| --- | --- |
| Vessel arrives | Arrival notice issued |
| Importer clears customs | Customs declaration + release |
| Release obtained | D/O issued against originals/telex/waybill |
| Pickup | Terminal gate out against D/O |
