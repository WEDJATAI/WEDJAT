# Egypt — Company Types and Legal Entity Structuring Guide

## Entity landscape overview

Egypt is a civil-law jurisdiction. Private investment companies are regulated primarily by **Companies Law No. 159 of 1981** (as amended, notably by **Law No. 4 of 2018**), alongside the Investment Law framework; the **General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI)** is the one-stop registration authority for investment companies. Partnership forms derive from the Commercial Code. Egypt also operates **free zones** (public and private) under GAFI with their own licensing regime.

## Main entity forms

| Form (Arabic / local) | Legal form | Liability | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LLC — Limited Liability Company (Sharikat al-Mas'uliyyah al-Mahdudah)** | Private company | Limited to capital | Most common trading/investment vehicle; Law 159 as amended 2018 removed prior minimum-capital requirements; managed by at least one manager (no board required) |
| **SAE — Joint-Stock Company (Sharikat al-Mossahamah)** | Public-style company ≈ plc | Limited | Historically minimum capital E£250,000 (law-driven; confirm current thresholds with GAFI); board of at least three directors; used for larger ventures and those seeking listing |
| **OPC — One Person Company** | Single-shareholder company | Limited | Newly introduced by Law No. 4 of 2018; equity cannot be listed or traded on the stock exchange (DLA Piper) |
| **Sharikat Tadamun (شركة تضامن)** | General partnership | Unlimited, joint | Partners (natural persons typically) |
| **Sharikat Tawssiyah Bassita (شركات توصية بسيطة)** | Simple limited partnership | Mixed (GP unlimited, LP limited) | |
| **Sharikat Tawssiyah bel-Ashom** | Limited liability partnership variant | Mixed | |
| **Sole proprietorship** | Individual | Unlimited | Registered in the commercial register |

## Foreign company presence options

- **LLC or SAE subsidiary** with GAFI registration (most common foreign structure; 100% foreign ownership permitted in most sectors outside negative lists).
- **Branch of a foreign company** — registered with GAFI (or the relevant ministry for some sectors); can contract locally, generally limited to executing the parent's contracts.
- **Representative office** — market study/promotion only; cannot trade.
- **Free-zone company (public/private free zones)** — licensed under the Investment Law; export-oriented operations, customs advantages.

## Registration process overview (GAFI route)

1. Name reservation and incorporation approval at **GAFI** (one-stop shop).
2. Notarized **memorandum & articles** (Arabic; standard forms per company type).
3. Capital deposit + bank certificate where applicable; commercial register extraction.
4. **Tax card** registration and VAT registration as applicable.
5. Sector licenses (import/export cards — see below; industrial licenses via IDM for factories).

## Trade-specific structuring notes

- **Importer registration**: commercial importers must be registered with **GOEIC (General Organization for Export and Import Control)**; natural-person importers must be of Egyptian nationality, registered in the commercial register and holding a tax card (per GOEIC's published requirements). An **exporter card** is required for customs clearance of exports.
- Foreign-invested trading companies face minimum requirements (e.g., historical E£ threshold / Egyptian-partner considerations for retail trade; confirm current rules with GAFI/GOEIC).
- Free-zone entities are generally export-oriented; selling into the local market requires customs treatment as import.
