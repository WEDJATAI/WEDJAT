# United States — Import and Export Customs Documentation Guide

## Customs administration

**U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)** administers imports under the Tariff Act; the **HTS (Harmonized Tariff Schedule)** classifies goods (10-digit US extension of the universal HS — see the HS reference). Export control involves the Census Bureau's **AES** and licensing agencies (BIS/ DDTC). Entries are filed by licensed customs brokers under the importer's power of attorney, with the **Importer of Record (IOR)** liable for duties and declarations.

| Packing list | All | Exporter | |
| Bill of lading / air waybill | All | Carrier | |

## Import procedure overview

1. **Entry filing** within 15 calendar days of arrival (CF 3461/ electronic via ACE) with the broker under the IOR's bond.
2. **CBP Form 7501 Entry Summary** must be filed and **estimated duties deposited within 10 working days after entry** (CBP); optional entry/summary-at-once filing per 19 CFR 142.
3. Examination/release; **single entry** is the import event; **entry summary + duty** close it within the 10-day window.
4. Post-entry: protests, prior disclosure, reconciliations; **CBP Form 28/29** requests/ demands.

## Export procedure overview

1. Determine ECCN/ license requirements; obtain licenses if controlled.
2. File **EEI in AES** (USPPI) before export for covered shipments; record ITN on documents.
3. Deliver to carrier with invoice/ SLI; carrier files export manifest data.
4. Retain EEI documentation 5 years (Census/ FTR rules).

## Duty and tax framework

- **Duties**: HTS column-1 general/ special (FTA) rates; AD/CVD cash deposits where applicable; **de minimis** threshold for low-value imports (Section 321 — verify current levels).
- **MPF/ HMF** merchandise-processing and harbor-maintenance fees on entries.
- No federal VAT; state sales taxes are outside customs.

## Special requirements and trade programs

- **Importer of Record** + customs bond structure (see the US entities guide).
- **Forced-labor (UFLPA)** documentation/ supply-chain tracing for risk goods.
- FTAs: USMCA, KORUS etc. with certification of origin; **AGOA** preferences for African-origin goods (relevant to Egypt/Kenya shippers — Egypt has no US FTA; QIZ rules for apparel).
- **CTPAT** trusted-trader; **ACE** single window for all agency filings.
